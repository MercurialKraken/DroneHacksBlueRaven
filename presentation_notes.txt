3. SIMULATION vs REALITY - WHAT SURPRISED US (good material: "why a sim pass isn't a real pass")
===========================================================================================================
Found in the T4 simulator, fixed before flying:
- A 90 deg corner at 0.15 m/s became a yaw in place: cuVSLAM lost ~14 m (no parallax; the real drone's VIO has no
  IMU fusion). Fix: a gradual 3-target ARC (radius 0.7 m), turning while moving, yaw capped at 30 deg/s.
- Speed and yaw cap are coupled: 0.3 m/s + 30 deg/s crashed into a wall beside the window in the sim (the nose lagged
  the turn); 0.15 + 30 and 0.3 + 45 passed. Rule of thumb (Darion): corner radius >= speed / yaw rate.
- The drone moves toward its NOSE more than toward the commanded velocity (Darion, from the sim bags).

Found only on the REAL drone (the sim did not show them):
- Take-off drift: ~0.38 m back-left during the 3.8 s climb. Real motion (Hinson: the room's wall stayed still in the
  depth images), not a VIO jump. The first attempt landed early on it (0.46 m > the 0.40 m safety guard).
- A steady ~0.22 m offset to the LEFT of the direction of travel for the whole route; refnav never closed it, and
  because it is > its 0.20 m "wait" threshold the reference kept waiting: 74 s instead of ~20 s (0.04-0.05 m/s).
- PX4 achieved only ~15% of the commanded velocity along the command; the shortfall is a steady body-frame push
  ~0.10 m/s left + ~0.06 m/s back. In the sim, with the same code and route: 105%, offset 0.05 m.
- Ruled out (with numbers): PX4 heading vs VIO (within 1 deg), PX4's velocity estimate (matches VIO motion), the
  flight interface (passes the command straight through). The estimate was consistent: PX4 and VIO within 4 cm.
- On the ground before arming, PX4 read a steady tilt of 1.2 deg right-down / 0.9 deg nose-down: either the floor at
  the launch mark is not level, or the IMU level calibration/mounting is off (a 1 deg bias is enough for 0.1 m/s).
  Cheap check: a phone level on the floor and on the drone.
- Integration lessons: depth subscribed "best effort" got 2 frames in 60 s across processes on the drone (RELIABLE:
  677); MPPI in refnav's single thread delayed odometry (35 -> 55 ms); every launcher failure that night came from
  ros2 CLI calls hanging on a cold ROS daemon after a reboot (the launcher now uses process counts and logs only).

Hypotheses for the left offset (open):
- H1 (Chelsea): the take-off disturbance shifts the drone and the error is carried along.
- H2 (Arnav): control / PIDs - PX4 doesn't deliver the commanded velocity, and refnav's correction is proportional
  only, so a steady 0.11 m/s shortfall leaves 0.11 / 0.5 = 0.22 m - exactly the offset seen.
- Check of H1 on flight 160449 (one flight only - the push may not repeat): refnav DID fly out the backward part of
  the take-off drift (-0.35 -> ~0 m in ~4 s), but the left part grew (0.19 -> 0.29 m) while it was being pulled back,
  and after the turn the drone sat 0.20 m FORWARD of the path (left of its new direction), not where a carried-over
  take-off offset would put it. So in that flight the take-off drift and the steady offset look like two symptoms of
  one push. route1-chelsea's 10 s hold over the take-off point tests H1 directly on the next flight.
